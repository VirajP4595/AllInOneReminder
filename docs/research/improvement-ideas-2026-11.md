# Fix trust-breaking friction before adding features

AllInOneReminder already offers more than most free competitors: family profiles, meal-linked timing, PRN caps, stock, appointments, a widget, TTS and encrypted backup, all offline. Its weakest points are not missing features. They are small bugs and pieces of friction in the code that make the app feel unreliable or hard to use for older users. The Today screen snooze ignores the user's default-snooze setting. A debug "seed data" button can inject fake profiles into a real medicine list. Times can only be set with ±30-minute buttons. Sound settings are stored but never used. Nothing can be undone, so a mis-tapped "Taken" hides a dose that was never taken. The evidence supports fixing these first. Reminder apps roughly double the odds of self-reported adherence, but **no specific behaviour-change feature has been shown to drive that effect** ([BMJ Open meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7045248)). Older users and caregivers rank **simple setup and use** above everything else ([JMIR 2020](https://www.jmir.org/2020/12/e18074/XML)). The recommended order is: (1) about a dozen small fixes (mostly effort S) that close bugs and spec gaps the audit found; (2) a "Next" tier focused on honest logging, setup speed, a caregiver-set Simple mode and alarm reliability; (3) a "Later" tier of offline extras. Server, SMS and Play-Services features stay out of scope. Gamification should become more forgiving rather than more elaborate, because there is **no pooled evidence that it improves medication adherence** and streaks have documented harms.

Two of these fixes affect safety, not just convenience. **Undo (#9) protects the medication record.** Once a dose is marked "Taken", the double-dose guard and the history treat it as taken, so a mis-tap hides a missed dose. Round Health users complain about the related problem that "taken later" is logged at the tap time rather than the real time ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/round-health/id1059591124)). The undo and edit-taken-time path should be one feature. Any change that affects pending alarms must go through `AlarmCoordinator`, as CLAUDE.md requires. **The editable Wake/Snack/Bed anchors (#5) fix a hidden bug.** Today a "30 min after waking" reminder rings at 07:30 for every profile, whatever that person's routine. A process fix belongs with these as well. DECISIONS.md records "Cease writing new unit/integration tests" (2026-11-06), which conflicts with CLAUDE.md's "never skip or weaken tests". Every feature built after that date, including stock, appointments, the widget, TTS and Health Connect, has relied on manual checks ([docs/DECISIONS.md](../docs/DECISIONS.md)). The quick-win PRs should add tests for the snooze, undo and anchor fixes and should restart test-first work in `/core/schedule`.

## Reliable alarms and honest logs matter more than new features

The adherence research gives a modest but sobering picture. Across 9 RCTs, apps improved self-reported adherence (**OR 2.12, 95% CI 1.64–2.75**), but meta-regression found **no link between the behaviour-change techniques used and effect size** ([BMJ Open 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7045248)). The NIHR adds that apps are evaluated too inconsistently to compare features ([NIHR](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/apps-to-help-patients-take-medication-on-time-need-to-be-evaluated-in-a-consistent-way)). The working conclusion is that the value lies in reminders that fire reliably, are easy to confirm and leave an accurate log. Extra features do not add measurable benefit on their own. Competitor reviews point the same way: after paywalls, the most common complaints are silenced or missed notifications and needing Wi-Fi ([MoneySavingExpert forum](https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6645234/medisafe-app-no-longer-free-from-january-1st-2026)).

Alarm fatigue sets the limit on persistence. In clinical systems, **acceptance fell about 30% with each additional reminder in the same episode** ([PMC5387195](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5387195/)), and **72.5% of alerts were dismissed in under 3 seconds** ([PMC8892274](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8892274)). These are clinical rather than consumer studies, but they argue against AIOR's current behaviour of re-ringing every 5 minutes with no visible cap. A better design is a short escalation ladder with a cap: ring, then one or two louder re-rings, then mark the dose "Missed" and show it on Today for the caregiver. Snoozing should show the count ("Snoozed 2 of 3"). After the last allowed snooze, the user should have to choose Taken or Skip. Older adults "especially desired a sense of control over their notifications" ([Graphics Interface 2018](https://graphicsinterface.org/wp-content/uploads/gi2018-19.pdf)), so the ladder needs sensible defaults by criticality (medicine escalates, water does not) with a per-item override.

The platform side is mostly sound. A single `setAlarmClock` plus a full-screen intent is the strongest option Android offers, because the system "never adjusts" these alarms and wakes from low-power modes to deliver them ([Android docs](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule)). The remaining gaps found in the audit are about recovery. There is no automatic WorkManager retry after a coordinator failure, which review R2-6 asked for. Some background paths still swallow every exception ([security review](../docs/reviews/security-review-2026-11.md)). The health check has no Do Not Disturb row, although spec §16 requires one, and it has no guide for Huawei/Honor/Motorola. On the Android 11 Realme target, the risk to plan for is ColorOS's battery manager. Realme devices are reported to delay alarms by up to about 15 minutes under some settings. The usual workarounds are Auto-launch, Background activity and battery "Don't optimize" ([Suntimes recommendations](https://forrestguice.codeberg.page/Suntimes/help/alarms/recommendations/)). The existing Oppo/Realme text guide should be checked against ColorOS 11.1 menu paths, where Auto-launch now sits under App Management ([AirDroid guide](https://biz-help.airdroid.com/en/support/solutions/articles/154000200219--connection-oppo-devices-how-to-keep-biz-daemon-running-in-the-background-)). The health check should also gain an "alarm health" self-check that warns when the last alarm fired late or the next one is not registered.

## Roadmap: Now, Next, Later and Not doing

The ranking weighs three things: user value for a caregiver running one shared phone for older parents, the strength of the adherence or usability evidence, and effort. Every item works offline at $0 without INTERNET unless it is flagged. Many items are Phase 3/4 features under PLAN.md, so each one needs a DECISIONS.md entry, as earlier scope deviations already have.

### Later: offline extras worth doing once the base is solid

Several extras are worth doing later, and all are offline. **Cyclic on/off schedules** (21/7), a **weekend-time shift** and **configurable weekend days** are pure `/core/schedule` logic. medTimer, the closest offline open-source analogue, offers them ([medTimer](https://github.com/Futsch1/medTimer)). The current enum allows only a Saturday/Sunday weekend. **Expiry dates on stock** are another medTimer feature. **Reminder windows**, which alert at the start, middle and end of a window as Round Health does, are a third option. **Per-profile quiet hours** with a critical-medicine bypass would help, though no competitor evidence was found for them. Other candidates are **offline TTS in a per-profile language**, **Hindi localization** via AppCompat's per-app locale backport (which works on Android 11 but requires moving MainActivity to `AppCompatActivity`) ([App languages guide](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/app-languages)), a **Today layout choice** (timeline vs per-person columns), **custom item categories** and **remembered custom units**. Dynamic colour and themed icons are nearly free, but they need Android 12+/13+, so the Realme target will never show them. A **CSV medicine-list/adherence export** is the most-requested "premium" capability among competitors ([medTimer](https://github.com/Futsch1/medTimer); [MyTherapy](https://www.mytherapyapp.com/diabetes-blood-glucose-app)). However, the owner closed the PDF report (#34) and vitals (#32) as "not wanted". They should be reopened only if the owner decides to, perhaps limited to a plain medicine list for doctor visits.

If wrist delivery matters, post a separate normal notification with a stable dismissal ID, and make the Taken receiver idempotent ([Wear bridging](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/notifications/bridger)).

## Gamification and customization need guardrails for older users

AIOR's points, levels, streaks and badges rest on weaker evidence than the app's prominence gives them. Gamification has a small-to-medium effect on **physical activity** (**Hedges g = 0.42** across 16 studies; [JMIR 2022](https://doaj.org/article/9754f1fd9dd84266b5e5a30336858ad8)). A 2022 scoping review found **no evidence synthesis at all on its efficacy for medication adherence** ([PMC8902658](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8902658/)). The best-known gamified adherence app, Mango Health, was acquired and has disappeared ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/mango-health-acquired-pharma-service-trialcard)). The documented harms are concrete. Streaks foster dependence, and users who drop the app often drop the behaviour ([CHI'16 study](https://core.ac.uk/download/79500911.pdf), small qualitative study). Fitness-app users report **demotivation, shame and disconnection** when health is reduced to numbers ([News-Medical on BJHP 2025](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20251022/Study-reveals-the-negative-behavioral-and-psychological-consequences-of-fitness-apps.aspx)). Almost half of surveyed eating/fitness-app users reported a negative experience ([JMIR mHealth 2019](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/6/e14239/PDF)).

For a medicine app, the main risk is that **rewards create an incentive to log falsely**. If "Taken" earns points and an honest "Skipped" breaks a streak, the system rewards tapping Taken. That corrupts the log the double-dose guard depends on. The fixes are cheap. Award points for responding (Taken, or Skipped with a reason), not only for Taken. Never reward PRN or extra doses. Replace hard streaks with forgiving ones ("6 of 7 days", one grace day a week). Keep medicine neutral, with no confetti and no "you broke your streak" copy. Wellness items such as water and walks can stay playful. Add a per-profile "Hide points & badges" switch, since some parents will find the game childish. The current rules (10 points for an on-time habit, 5 for others, with a 30-minute on-time window, in [Points.kt](../core/gamify/src/main/kotlin/app/allinonereminder/gamify/Points.kt)) should be checked against these principles. The audit did not confirm whether a skip earns anything. Copy should be neutral ("Missed at 9:00 — mark now?"), not moralising.

Customization needs the same restraint. No study ranks customization features by adherence benefit. The evidence supports two narrower claims: users want **control over notifications**, and products must be **simple to set up** ([GI 2018](https://graphicsinterface.org/wp-content/uploads/gi2018-19.pdf); [JMIR 2020](https://www.jmir.org/2020/12/e18074/XML)). Older users also struggle with strict input formats ([ZURB summarising NN/g](https://radar.zurb.com/article/design-for-seniors-ship-a-better-product-for-everyone)). That is why typed dates and ±30-minute buttons are real problems, not cosmetic ones. The right model is layered. Defaults should follow the phone (12/24h, theme). A few high-value, per-profile choices should be visible: colour and photo, "my sound", meal/routine times, and Simple mode. Everything else stays under a collapsed "More options". On a shared phone, the caregiver should be able to **lock a profile's settings behind a PIN** when Simple mode is on, so a parent cannot accidentally change a dose time while exploring. The guiding rule for each new option is to add it only if a caregiver would set it once and the parent would never need to touch it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The issue is not which features AIOR lacks compared with Medisafe; AIOR already beats most free tiers. The issue is whether every alarm fires, whether every log entry is true, and whether a 70-year-old can use the app without help. The quick wins, undo/edit, the escalation cap and Simple mode serve all three, and most are effort S. The constraints help here. "Free, no ads, no account, works offline" is now a differentiator after Medisafe's 2026 paywall ([Pillo blog](https://pillo.care/blog/medisafe-no-longer-free-best-free-alternatives), a competitor source), and almost every feature that conflicts with the constraints is one the owner has already deprioritised.

Two uncertainties remain. First, none of this has been tested with the actual parents. A one-hour session watching a parent set a time, snooze and undo on the Realme phone would check the priorities better than any further desk research. Second, the gamification evidence for medicine is close to nil in both directions. Treat the current system as an experiment the caregiver can switch off per profile. Do not build it out until real use on this family's phone shows it helps.
